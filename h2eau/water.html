Step 1 · Awareness

"Clean" isn't always clean.

Most of us believe we're drinking clean water. Here's what's actually going on with the three most common choices.

🚰 Tap

Every city is different, which is why a report for your zip code matters.

  • PFAS "forever chemicals"
  • Disinfection byproducts
  • Heavy metals
  • Microplastics

🧴 Bottled

It's not just the bottle. Particles come from processing and even twisting the cap.

  • About 240,000 plastic particles per liter (Qian et al., PNAS, 2024)
  • Often the same source as tap
  • Adds up to $1,000+ a year for a family

🫗 Filtered

Better than nothing, and reverse osmosis is great at removing contaminants.

  • Takes the bad out
  • Doesn't add anything back
  • No molecular hydrogen

Step 2 · Understanding

Not all water is created equal.

The water I drink is ERW, electrolyzed reduced water, made at home from my tap by an Enagic machine. Here's what makes it different.

It's not just alkaline

Alkaline water only changes pH. ERW also carries molecular hydrogen (H₂) and a negative ORP, which is what gives it its antioxidant quality. pH alone doesn't do what most people think.

The smallest molecule

Molecular hydrogen is the smallest molecule there is, and it's been studied for decades, especially in Japan, for how it works with oxidative stress in the body.

Why the machine matters

How much hydrogen your water carries depends on the plates inside the machine. Enagic has been making them in Japan for 50+ years, and their factories are ISO 9001, 14001 and 13485 certified.

Common questions

Things people usually ask.

Is this just alkaline water?

No, and it's the most important difference. Alkaline water only changes pH. ERW also carries molecular hydrogen and a negative ORP. Plenty of "alkaline" products don't have either.

I already use a Brita or reverse osmosis. Isn't that enough?

Filtering is better than nothing, and RO is really good at taking contaminants out. It just doesn't add anything back. Think of it as removing the bad versus adding something good. Both matter, and I'm happy to help you figure out what makes sense for your water.

What if I can't afford a machine right now?

That's completely okay. There are options for every budget: smaller starting points, financing, and in some areas ways to try the water without owning a machine. Message me and I'll share honest options, not one thing pushed on everyone.
